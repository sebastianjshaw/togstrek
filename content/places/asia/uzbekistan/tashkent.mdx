The capital where Soviet architecture meets modern Uzbekistan. Skyscrapers, mosaic mausoleums, a tower with an observation deck, and a bazaar. Full write-up from the 2024 Silk Road leg to follow.

## Sights & Culture

### Tashkent Metro

The metro is one of the better reasons to be in Tashkent. Each station was designed by a different architect and the results range from impressive to extraordinary. Five stops worth making: Alisher Navoi, decorated with scenes from the poet's works; Mustakillik Maydoni, the Independence Square station; Kosmonavtlar, with its space-age cosmonaut theme; Paxtakor, named for the cotton growers and tiled in a cotton-flower mosaic; and Tashkent station itself. Cheap, fast, and worth riding for the architecture alone.

### Earthquake Monument

A monument to the 1966 earthquake that reshaped the city. The sculpture shows a figure protecting his family, our guide noted that the woman is doing as much as the man, which the official framing tends to understate.

![Canon EOS R5 EF24-105mm f/4L IS USM, 32mm, f9, 1/250, ISO200](https://media.togstrek.com/asia/uzbekistan/tashkent/Earthquake%20Memorial%20-%20001.jpg)


### Hazrati Imam Complex

One of the significant Islamic complexes in Tashkent, containing the Mausoleum of Saint Abu Bakr Kaffal Shashi, an 11th-century reconstruction over a 10th-century tomb. The saint was a hadith scholar of some importance. The blue domes here are part of the broader Timurid colour scheme, Amir Timur chose the blue that now defines the skyline of Tashkent, Bukhara, and Samarkand. The guide also noted that the Taj Mahal was built by a descendant of Timur's line, and therefore represents a continuation of Timurid Islamic architecture. There's a kernel of truth: the Mughals were Timurid in origin, and Shah Jahan, who built the Taj Mahal, was Babur's grandson and Timur's great-great-great-grandson. The lineage is real; calling it Timur's direct architectural legacy is a stretch. One of the shining jewels is the Tillya Sheikh Mosque.

### Palace of Grand Duke Nikolai Konstantinovich

The Romanov palace in Tashkent, built for the exiled Grand Duke who was banished here after a scandal involving stolen family jewels. An incongruous piece of imperial Russia in Central Asia, and a good story.

![Canon EOS R5 EF24-105mm f/4L IS USM, 32mm, f4, 1/1000, ISO200](https://media.togstrek.com/asia/uzbekistan/tashkent/The%20palace%20of%20Grand%20Duke%20Nicholas%20Constantinovich%20-%20001.jpg)


### Cosmonaut Statue

A Soviet-era monument to the cosmonaut programme, consistent with the broader theme of space exploration that runs through Central Asian public sculpture from Bishkek onwards.
