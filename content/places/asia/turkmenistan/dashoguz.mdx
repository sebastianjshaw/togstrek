Dashoguz is the entry point into Turkmenistan from Uzbekistan and the base for the [Köneürgench](/asia/turkmenistan/kunya-urgench) visit. It's a provincial Turkmen city rather than a showpiece, though the showpiece instincts are present throughout. Gold statues, a book-shaped library, a congress hall with eight doors to heaven, and the president's face on posters beside the gold. Turkmenistan is one of the world's top countries for natural gas reserves, and the money that implies has gone into public architecture of a very particular kind.

## Sights & Culture

### Gold Statue: First President

A large gold statue of Turkmenistan's first president, Saparmurat Niyazov, with a white poster of the current president positioned to his right. The succession is clearly communicated.

### Gold Statue: Second President

A second gold statue, this one of Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow, the second president and father of the current incumbent. Turkmenistan has a pattern here.

### Baýdak meydançasy

A significant flagpole in the civic centre, consistent with the Turkmen preference for very tall flagpoles.

### Ruhyýet Congress Hall

A brown-domed building serving as both congress hall and spiritual centre. The eight doors of the building correspond to the eight doors to heaven in Turkmen cosmology, a detail that sits interestingly between Islamic and older Turkic traditions.

### City Library

A large blue building shaped like an open book. Turkmenistan's public buildings tend towards the literal in their architectural symbolism, and this is a committed example.

Identifiable by the green star above the entrance. Notable primarily because in Turkmenistan even a pension office gets an architectural statement.

### Bay Town

Dinner on arrival. One of the options we chose was Gold leaf steak, which is the kind of menu choice that locates you in Turkmenistan immediately. Good, but not great.

### Oguz Restaurant

A restaurant near the civic centre, we stopped here during the tour.
