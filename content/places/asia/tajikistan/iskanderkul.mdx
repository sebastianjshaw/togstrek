Iskanderkul is a mountain lake, and a beautiful one. It is named for Alexander the Macedonian, who is said to have loved this place. On the drive in, to the small lakefront hotel and restaurant we were stopping at for lunch, the view was good enough to stop for twice. After lunch and a walk along the lake shore, we did a fairly strenuous hike, and came back with some panic, having apparently lost a hiker. It turned out he'd told one of the people searching for him that he'd gone back to the van, and the searcher hadn't felt that was information worth passing on.

## Sights & Culture

### Toll Crossing

The official toll collection on the approach over the bridge is a man with a piece of string. It is, in its way, efficient.

### Iskanderkul View Point

The most distant of the viewpoints we stopped at. From up here the river is a pencil-thin ribbon snaking along the valley floor, the mountains rising steeply on either side.

## Sports & Activities

### Waterfall of the Fann Mountains

A long but relatively easy hike brings you to the Waterfall of the Fann Mountains. It's not Victoria Falls, but it's worth the walk. The water changes colour constantly as you follow the river: most often a snow-white froth over an almost beige flow, heavy with silt from the canyon walls; occasionally, where enough of it runs over smoothed stones, it turns a clear, vibrant blue.

## Restaurants & Bars

### Zumrad Restaurant

A hotel and restaurant on the lake serving simple local fare. It's decent rather than remarkable, but the view, more than anything on the plate, is the selling point.

![The Zumrad restaurant on the shore of Iskanderkul, the lake and mountains beyond](https://media.togstrek.com/asia/tajikistan/iskanderkul/Zumrad%20restaurant%20-%20001.jpg)
