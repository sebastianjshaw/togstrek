Tulum is a small town on the Caribbean coast of Mexico, known for its white sand beaches, hotels, clear water, and jungle with ancient ruins. It is one of the most visited spots on the Riviera — close to Playa del Carmen, but without the same “lads on tour” energy. The famous **Mayan ruins** sit on a cliff above the sea. The coast is also dotted with eco-minded stays, yoga, and wellness spots if that is your speed.

## Tulum Archaeological Zone

The Tulum Archaeological Zone is a Mayan complex on the Yucatán coast. It was an important trading port and is laid out across roughly 39 acres, with many buildings still easy to read on the ground.

### Temple of the Frescos

<div className="togstrek-place-mdx-photo-feature-wrap">

![Temple of the Frescos, Tulum](https://media.togstrek.com/north-america/mexico/tulum/Temple+of+the+Frescoes-20221223-0001.jpg)

</div>

The Temple of the Frescos takes its name from the **murals** on the inner walls — scenes from Maya mythology and a rare glimpse of how people here saw their gods and rituals. The plan is simple: a rectangular base, a narrow doorway, and a small inner room whose walls carry the painted figures. The colours and detail have held up remarkably well.

The building also shows the coastal Maya use of **stucco**, shell-finished surfaces, and decorative columns — a recognisable “coastal” flavour compared with sites further inland.

### The Castle

The Castle (**El Castillo**) is the largest structure at Tulum. It likely combined **ceremony**, elite residence, and defence, with the sea on one side and land approaches on the other. The main mass sits highest; smaller volumes beside it are read as watch and storage spaces.

The stonework mixes **Maya and central Mexican** traits — limestone, stucco, and a roofline that stands out from simpler houses nearby.

### House of the Halach Uinic

A smaller building near the castle is associated with a **Halach Uinic** (“real man” / high ruler): a formal entrance, carved detail, and a blend of Maya and **Toltec-inspired** forms. It is usually interpreted as high-status residence rather than a public temple.

### The Temple of the Descending God

On the west side of the group, a small temple is named for a **stair relief** of the “descending” deity — often linked to fertility and renewal. The carving gives the building its modern name even when the upper masonry is badly weathered.

### The Temple of the Wind God

This temple is tied to **Kukulkan** / wind imagery and the constant breeze off the Caribbean. The masonry follows **Puuc** habits: careful cut stone and geometric panels on the façade, including a clear **Wind God** motif. Inside, a single vaulted room with a small opening is thought to have been used for ritual or meetings of the elite.

### House of the Columns

<div className="togstrek-place-mdx-photo-feature-wrap">

![House of the Columns](https://media.togstrek.com/north-america/mexico/tulum/House+of+the+Columns-20221223-0001.jpg)

</div>

The House of the Columns is a **rectangular range** supported by many columns — interpreted as elite housing. The columns carry **glyphs and reliefs** that echo the same political and religious themes as the larger temples.

### Tulum beach

The public beach below the ruins is an easy **swim break** after walking the site. The limestone **cliffs** behind the sand are the same formation the city was built on, so the horizon reads as one continuous edge of rock and turquoise water.

## Charlie's

**Charlie's** is run by Charlie — greying hippy and artist. It does not look like much from the street, but it opens into a **courtyard** built largely from recycled glass. A small corner shows some of his work; it is a pleasant place for a drink while waiting for a bus.

## Restaurant Bar Viva La Mexico

On **Avenida Tulum**, this restaurant has a huge dining room and serves **Mexican staples** at prices that are fair for the main drag and passing tour groups. Staff were friendly on our visit and the food was reliably good.

## Shopping

Tulum ranges from **handicraft stalls** and boutiques on Avenida Tulum to a few **designer** shops and natural skincare outlets. The main street is the default browse; the municipal market is the place for coffee, chocolate, and souvenirs — expect plenty of items that are generic to Mexico rather than unique to Tulum. Luxury labels also have a small footprint here if you want high-end fashion in the jungle.
