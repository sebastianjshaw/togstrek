Nestled at the base of a dramatic cliff along the tranquil Buna River, Blagaj is a picturesque village in Bosnia that captivates visitors with its natural beauty and rich cultural heritage. The village's serene atmosphere and welcoming locals make it an ideal destination for those seeking to explore the hidden gems of Bosnia while immersing themselves in the region's fascinating past.

## Sights & Culture

### Dervish House

One of the most iconic sights in Blagaj is the Dervish House, also known as Tekija or Tekke. This historic Sufi monastery, perched on the banks of the Buna River, is a striking example of Ottoman architecture and spiritual heritage. Constructed in the 16th century, the Dervish House has served as a place of worship and contemplation for centuries, welcoming dervishes and pilgrims seeking solace and connection with the divine.

If you have time, go inside, the beautifully adorned prayer rooms, adorned with intricate calligraphy and vibrant colours, exuded a sense of reverence and tranquillity. Exploring further, I discovered the charming courtyard, where the soothing sound of the river and the rustling leaves created an ambience perfect for reflection and meditation.

### Kula hercega Stjepana Vukcica Kosace

Another fascinating sight in Blagaj is the Kula Hercega Stjepana Vukčića Kosače, a medieval fortress that once belonged to the powerful Bosnian nobleman, Duke Stjepan Vukčić Kosača. Perched atop a hill overlooking the village, this tower offers a glimpse into the region's tumultuous past and the strategic importance of Blagaj during the Middle Ages.

## Parks & Gardens

### Buna River

The connection between the Dervish House and the Buna River is not only visual but also spiritual. The monastery was intentionally built at the base of the cliff where the river emerges, as the dervishes considered the location to be a sacred meeting point between the elements of earth and water. This harmonious union of nature and spirituality imbues the site with a profound sense of serenity and wonder.

In addition to its spiritual significance, the Buna River is also home to a fascinating network of caves carved into the limestone cliffs, used by the monks for meditation and storage.
