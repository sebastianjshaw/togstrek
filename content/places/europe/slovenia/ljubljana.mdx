Ljubljana's old town is walkable in an afternoon and the river is lined with cafés. Most of what you will remember was designed or redesigned by one architect, Jože Plečnik. Allow three days if you like museums, and they're quite helpfully clustered in most cases (By the Park, by the bus station and by the south end of the river). Ljubljana also makes a good base for day trips to [Postojna Cave and Predjama Castle](/europe/slovenia/postojna) and the coast at [Piran](/europe/slovenia/piran) as well as the famous Lake Bled.

Two things stand out about Slovenes. They love dogs and they love smoking, often at the same café table. English is spoken extremely well, to the point where you will rarely need anything beyond *hvala*.

## Sights & Culture

### Ljubljana Castle

The funicular up from the old town opened in 2006. The ride is short, about a minute, but it saves a steep walk, and one ticket covers the funicular, the castle and the attractions inside it, so doing the walk isn't a cost saving.

![Canon EOS R5 RF24-105mm F4 L IS USM, 24mm, f4.5, 1/125, ISO320](https://media.togstrek.com/europe/slovenia/ljubljana/Funicular%20-%20001.jpg)

The castle's own story is that it stands where St George fought the dragon, which, once killed, sank into a sudden cave. The guide who took us to the cave and castle has the dragon killed by Jason and the Argonauts instead, on their way back to the Adriatic. Looking into it, the city's own account offers both, plus a third in which the dragon is the Slavic god Veles. The St George version is the one that casts the dragon as pre-Christian belief, defeated by Christianity, which reads very much like a church overwriting the pagan history of the hill. Pick your myth; the dragon loses either way.

### Triple Bridge (Tromostovje)

Something of a unique marvel, the triple bridge is three bridges across the river linked to each other. Dating to the 13th century, it was redesigned by Jože Plečnik.

### Cobblers' Bridge

Also known as the Shoemaker's Bridge. It is decorated by two kinds of pillars, the Corinthian pillars which form the shape of the bridge itself and the Ionic pillars as lamp-bearers. It was designed by Jože Plečnik.

### National and University Library

A cool-looking building, and the windows look like books. Plečnik designed it in the early 1930s and it was built between 1936 and 1941.

![Canon EOS R5 RF24-105mm F4 L IS USM, 105mm, f8, 1/125, ISO200](https://media.togstrek.com/europe/slovenia/ljubljana/National%20and%20University%20Library%20-%20001.jpg)

### Križanke

Originally a monastery of the Teutonic Knights, with work believed to have begun in 1228. Plečnik converted it into an open-air festival venue in the 1950s, and it is now a music venue for the Ljubljana Festival and others. Beautiful architecture.

## Museums & Galleries

### Computer History Museum

Probably the best museum in Ljubljana. It is out of the centre, on Celovška cesta, and the [opening hours](https://www.racunalniski-muzej.si/en) are odd (afternoons on Thursday and Friday, daytime on Saturday at the time of writing), but it is well worth working around them. It was almost empty; and we were only the second visitors that day.

The ground floor has working versions of all sorts of computers and operating systems, and you can sit down and use them. I set the high score on *Bubble Bobble* and got the joy of hearing the screaching sounds of a ZX Spectrum reading a tape, something I'd not heard in perhaps 35 years. Upstairs was a temporary exhibition called *Hackers – Cyber (in)Security*.

### National Museum of Slovenia and Slovenian Museum of Natural History

Two museums sharing one building. The thing to find is the Divje Babe bone flute, which despite being the major draw for this musuem is a bit hidden. It is a cave-bear thigh bone with holes in it, around 50,000 to 60,000 years old, and the museum labels it a Neanderthal flute, the oldest musical instrument in the world. That claim is contested: several archaeologists argue the holes were made by a carnivore chewing the bone, not by anyone wanting a tune. The museum is confident; the debate is not settled. Either way, it is worth seeking out.

### National Museum of Contemporary History

In Tivoli Park. Not a huge museum, but good. Coverage of the break-up of Yugoslavia is thinner than I expected given that it is the defining event of their modern state, but the WW2 section is excellent. There is a section on gifts to the nation, which is a very odd selection of objects. Aparantly nations gift each other like a grandmother at Christmas.

Advertised to us as free on Google Maps, but it was €12 when we got there, so we skipped it. By this point in the trip, we'd seen a great deal of Plečnik. ## Restaurants & Bars

### Good Morning Vietnam

Vietnamese lunch. Good and huge portions.

### Marley & Me

A nice lunch. We sat in the window, which seemed to bring customers in after us, so consider it payment for the free advertising. The dish I had has [its own Reddit thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/food/comments/1eb370h/i_ate_traditional_idrian_dumplings_with_bacon/)

### Piazza

Pizza and ribeye steak. The inside is done up like a piazza, hence the name. Good ambience, good service and big portions.

### Coffee & Cocktails

A great café with good coffee.

### The Artisan

Great coffee, served in a glass jug with cups.

### Pekarna Pečjak

A bakery with coffee. The coffee was fine, it kept us out of the rain, and there is plenty of local produce on sale.

### Gig Bar & Burger

In the Supernova mall. I had the Philly cheesesteak, which was pretty good for a mall restaurant.

### Kavarna SEM

The lounge bar at the Ethnographic Museum. Very hipster, with high quality coffee as you'd expect.

### Das ist Walter

Sarajevo resturant and homage to the film "Walter Defends Sarajevo" which is on a loop on the wall. The food was great, and far too much for me to finish at lunchtime. I had the Pol, 5 čevapov and a sausage.
