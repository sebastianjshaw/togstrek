## Sights & Culture

### Stonehenge

#### 2016

It had been many years since I went to Stonehenge, and it has had a significant tourist upgrade with a small model village, and a walking track half way around the henge. Smaller than people expect, it remains an impressive Neolithic site.

![Canon Canon EOS 5D Mark III EF24-105mm f/4L IS USM, 24mm, f7.1, 1/200, ISO100](https://media.togstrek.com/europe/united-kingdom/england/salisbury/20161229-IMG_7662.jpg)

#### 2022

It was almost exactly five years before when I was last at [Stonehenge](/blog/stonehenge). It was a beautiful day when I was there last, but this time was much greyer and cloudier. It adds a certain something to the mysterious monoliths.

It remains a remarkable place. For the past thirty years, tourists must keep their distance. A walkway now fully circles the stones, giving you a chance to see the whole structure.

The site has been in some kind of use for around five thousand years, older even than [Hagar Qim](/blog/hagar-qim-and-mnajdra). Though Hagar Qim's structures are older than Stonehenge's stone structures.

### Salisbury Cathederal

Visible from miles around, the tall spire of Salisbury is iconic. It has been the tallest spire in the United Kingdom for around five hundred years. Inside is a museum offering insights into the history of Salisbury, but outside, the ancient Cathedral, originally started in 1220 stands proud. Salisbury Cathedral is the home of the Magna Carta, or at least one of the surviving copies. It is a very old cathedral and the central focus of the town of Salisbury. You can see it for miles. It’s not the first time I’ve been here, but it’s always an impressive sight and lends itself to memorable photographs. I was leaning heavily on HDR for many of these shots to capture the stained glass in particular.
